# First 30 days

1. Days 1–2. Create the real branches (head office, knitting unit, sewing unit, wash, warehouse). Turn off modules you will not use in month one. Keep issues, orders, yarn store and knitting on.
2. Days 2–4. Load buyers, suppliers, colours, sizes, yarn counts, machines and the defect names your QC already shouts. Do not rename them to look like software.
3. Days 4–6. Enter the open buyer POs and their ship dates. Confirm each one so the TNA is built backwards, skipping Friday and the holiday list.
4. Days 6–8. Approve the BOM (grams per piece, knit waste, link waste) before anyone books yarn. Run MRP and buy only the shortfall.
5. Days 8–12. Receive yarn only through a GRN. Incoming inspection is mandatory. A rejected shade stays out of the ledger.
6. Days 12–18. Knit against the job. Bundles get a barcode. Downtime reasons are the real ones: needle break, yarn break, no yarn, no operator, program, power.
7. Days 18–22. Final audit uses the buyer's AQL. A fail holds the job. Do not pack a held job.
8. Days 22–26. Packing list, gate pass, then the commercial invoice. Post the GRN voucher and check yarn inventory against the ledger.
9. Days 26–30. Payroll from attendance and piece rate. Read the open-issue board with the GM every morning. Close an issue only with cause, corrective action and preventive action.

Keep the paper challan for one month beside the screen. When the two match for a week, stop the paper.
